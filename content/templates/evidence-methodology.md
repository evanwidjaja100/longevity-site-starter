# Evidence Methodology

## How we gather evidence

Our evidence searches follow a structured protocol per content item. Searches cover:

- peer-reviewed databases (PubMed, Cochrane, etc.);
- grey literature (government reports, clinical registries);
- manufacturer documentation and public specifications.

Each search has a documented cutoff date. Sources published after that date are not included in the current review.

## How we grade evidence

Each material claim is assigned an evidence grade based on:

1. **Study design** — randomised trials carry more weight than observational studies or mechanistic reasoning.
2. **Consistency** — do multiple studies agree?
3. **Directness** — does the evidence measure the claimed outcome in the claimed population?
4. **Precision** — how certain is the effect estimate?
5. **Risk of bias** — are there methodological concerns?

Grades use the scale defined in the [Editorial Policy](/editorial-policy/).

## Confidence scoping

Overall article confidence reflects the most material claims. A claim with lower confidence may appear in the same article as a stronger claim; each is labelled separately.

## Original contribution

When we conduct original testing, the protocol is registered before testing begins. Results are recorded in an approved test record and linked to the published review.

## Study appraisal

Key studies cited in our content are appraised using a standard worksheet covering design, sample, bias, duration, sponsorship, and relevance to the guide scope.
