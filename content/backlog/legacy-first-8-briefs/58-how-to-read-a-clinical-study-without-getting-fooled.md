# Brief 58: How to Read a Clinical Study Without Getting Fooled

- **Primary keyword:** how to read clinical study
- **Intent:** Informational / research literacy guide
- **Planned order:** 58
- **Medical review:** Yes

## Reader outcome
Help a reader develop the skills to read and critically evaluate a clinical study, using accurate examples and avoiding misinterpretation.

## Required sections
- Define study types and their hierarchy of evidence
- Create a step-by-step reading framework: abstract, methods, results, discussion
- Separate statistical significance from practical significance
- Give guidance on spotting bias, conflicts, and overinterpretation
- List red flags for misleading study reporting

## Evidence and originality requirements
- Use current official documentation for product and regulatory facts.
- Use primary research and current systematic reviews for health claims.
- Log every material claim and its evidence note.
- Add original testing, diagrams, interviews, or decision frameworks.
- State uncertainty, population limits, and conflicts before conclusions.

## Release gate
- Medical reviewer confirms research literacy boundaries.
- Affiliate disclosure before the first monetized link.
- Editor approves non-sensational title/body consistency.
- Assign a 6- or 12-month recheck.
